RADSDF MODELING GUIDE
RAD designs are Python programs written with the radsdf API. RAD runs a program in its sandboxed worker; the program builds an expression graph of immutable shapes, and RAD's native kernel evaluates each part's signed distance field (negative inside, zero on the surface, positive outside, in mm) and meshes it with sharp-feature dual contouring for the preview, measurements and 3MF export.

Program contract
- Assign the final geometry to `result`: one 3D shape (one part: id "solid/0", name "Part 1"), a list or tuple of 3D shapes (one part each: "solid/<i>", "Part <i+1>"), or an Assembly (named parts and folders, below). Extrude, revolve or loft 2D profiles first.
- Preloaded names: every public radsdf and assembly name listed in this guide, `sdf` (the radsdf module), `math`, `np` (numpy), `rad` (rad.print) and `imported_assets` (imported meshes by asset ID; see Imported meshes). Ordinary Python works: functions, loops, comprehensions, numpy.
- The program runs without file, network, subprocess or GUI access; put every dimension in the program. Keep it deterministic (no clock or unseeded randomness) and readable: named parameters, helper functions and loops for repeated features.
- Mistakes raise SDFError (a ValueError) with a message; the build reports the program line.

Example
```python
plate = box((80, 50, 6), center=(0, 0, 3), radius=1)
web = box((6, 50, 40), center=(-37, 0, 23), radius=1)
body = union(plate, web, smooth=3)                      # 3 mm fillet where the web meets the plate
holes = [cylinder(3.2, 20, center=(x, 0, 3)) for x in (-10, 25)]
bore = cylinder(6, 20, center=(-37, 0, 30), axis='x')   # horizontal bore through the web
result = Assembly("Bracket")
result.add(difference(body, *holes, bore), id="bracket", name="Bracket", color="#7A8B99")
```

Conventions
- Millimetres and degrees; right-handed rotations; Z is up.
- 3D primitives are centred on `center` (default origin). cylinder, cone and torus run along `axis` (default +Z; 'x', 'y', 'z', '-x', '-y', '-z' or a vector).
- 2D profiles lie in the XY plane. extrude(h) spans z = 0..h (center=True: -h/2..h/2). revolve() turns a profile about the Z axis: profile x is the radius and profile y becomes Z, so draw revolve profiles at x >= 0.
- Vectors are tuples, lists or numpy arrays; translate also takes separate numbers. Sizes and radii are one number for every axis or one per axis.
- Shapes are immutable: every operation returns a new shape, and reusing a shape (for example in several placements) stores it once.

3D primitives
- sphere(radius, center=(0, 0, 0))
- box(size, center=(0, 0, 0), radius=0): edge lengths size (number or (x, y, z)); radius rounds every edge and corner, at most half the smallest size.
- cylinder(radius, height, center=(0, 0, 0), axis=(0, 0, 1)): capped, centred along axis.
- cone(radius1, radius2, height, center=(0, 0, 0), axis=(0, 0, 1)): frustum with radius1 at the -axis end and radius2 at the +axis end; either radius may be 0.
- capsule(a, b, radius): all points within radius of the segment from a to b.
- torus(major_radius, minor_radius, center=(0, 0, 0), axis=(0, 0, 1))
- ellipsoid(radii, center=(0, 0, 0)): axis-aligned semi-axes.
- half_space(normal=(0, 0, 1), point=(0, 0, 0)): everything on the side opposite normal; unbounded, so only intersect it or subtract it.

2D profiles (XY plane)
- circle(radius, center=(0, 0))
- rect(size, center=(0, 0), radius=0): side lengths; radius rounds the corners.
- polygon(points): a simple polygon through [(x, y), ...], closed automatically, either orientation; at least three distinct points. The outline must not cross, touch or overlap itself (no repeated corner, no point on another edge): list the points in order along it, reversing pieces such as arcs that run the other way. Otherwise polygon() fails at its line, naming the points.
- regular_polygon(sides, radius, center=(0, 0), angle=0): corners on a circle of radius, the first at angle degrees.
- slot(a, b, radius): a stadium around the segment from a to b.
- ellipse(radii, center=(0, 0))
- half_plane(normal=(0, 1), point=(0, 0)): unbounded.
- arc_points(center, radius, start_angle, end_angle, segments=None): numpy (segments + 1, 2) points along an arc, to build polygons with arcs and fillets.
- involute_gear(teeth, module, pressure_angle=20, clearance=0.25, backlash=0, points_per_flank=12): spur gear profile centred at the origin with a tooth on +X; pitch radius module·teeth/2, tip radius pitch + module, 6 to 400 teeth.

Profiles to solids (methods of 2D profiles)
- p.extrude(height, center=False, taper=0): taper offsets the top profile by taper mm (negative narrows, for draft).
- p.extrude_between(z0, z1)
- p.revolve(offset=0): about Z; offset is added to the radius.
- p.loft_to(other, height, center=False): linear blend from p at the bottom to other at the top.
- 2D only: p.outline(thickness) (a band centred on the boundary), p.rotate(angle, center=(0, 0)), p.mirror(normal=(1, 0), point=(0, 0)), p.mirrored_copy(normal, point), p.polar_array(count, center=(0, 0), angle=360).

Booleans and blends (2D with 2D, 3D with 3D)
- union(*shapes, smooth=0), intersection(*shapes, smooth=0), difference(base, *cutters, smooth=0) take shapes or lists of shapes. smooth > 0 blends every join of that call with roughly that radius: a fillet for union, a rounded cut for difference and intersection.
- Operators a | b (union), a & b (intersection), a - b (difference) and methods a.union(...), a.intersection(...), a.difference(...).

Transforms (2D and 3D unless noted)
- s.translate(v) or s.translate(x, y, z); s.moved is the same.
- s.rotate(angle, axis=(0, 0, 1), center=(0, 0, 0)); s.rotate_x(angle, center), s.rotate_y(...), s.rotate_z(...) (3D).
- s.orient(direction, up=(0, 0, 1)): turn the shape so its up direction points along direction (3D).
- s.scale(factor, center=None): uniform, or one factor per axis.
- s.transform(matrix): a 4x4 or 3x4 matrix of rotation, uniform positive scale and translation (3D).
- s.mirror(normal=(1, 0, 0), point=(0, 0, 0)); s.mirrored_copy(normal, point) is the shape plus its reflection.
- rotation_matrix(angle, axis=(0, 0, 1)): a 3x3 numpy rotation.

Modifiers (3D; offset also 2D)
- s.offset(distance): grow (positive) or shrink (negative) every surface; growing rounds convex edges.
- s.shell(thickness, side="inside"): a closed hollow wall following the surface. "inside" keeps the outer surface (wall grows inward), "outside" keeps the inner surface, "center" straddles it. Open it with difference() or cut().
- s.cut(normal=(0, 0, 1), point=(0, 0, 0), keep="below"): keep the side of a plane opposite normal ("below") or along it ("above").
- s.elongate(x, y, z): split at the centre planes and insert straight sections 2·amount long, stretching a rounded shape without distorting its rounds.
- s.twist(degrees_per_mm): twist about Z (bounded shapes only).

Patterns (unioned copies, at most 10,000)
- s.linear_array(count, step): copies displaced by step from the previous one.
- s.grid_array(counts, steps): one count and one step per axis.
- s.polar_array(count, axis=(0, 0, 1), center=(0, 0, 0), angle=360) (3D): a full turn divides evenly, a partial arc puts copies at both ends.

Queries (call the native kernel; use sparingly in programs)
- s.bounds: conservative Bounds with lo, hi, size, center, finite, empty, union(b), intersection(b), expanded(d), corners(), as_list(). Blends and offsets make them slightly larger than the surface.
- s.evaluate(points) or s(points): signed distances; s.contains(points); s.lipschitz.
- 3D: s.gradient(points); s.raycast(origins, directions, max_distance=100000, tolerance=0.0001, max_steps=512) returns (hits, distances); s.mesh(...) (RAD meshes results itself).
- Low-level helpers programs rarely need: gradient(shape, points), raycast(shape, ...), kernel_version(), auto_cell_size(bounds, cells_across=200, minimum=0.02), serialize(roots), Graph, Mesh, merge_meshes, Bounds, SDF3, SDF2, SDFError.

Custom fields
- `x`, `y` and `z` are symbolic coordinates (Field objects). Arithmetic on them records an expression the kernel evaluates natively: `+ - * /`, unary `-`, `abs()`, `** number`, and sin, cos, exp, log, sqrt, atan2(y, x), minimum(*values, smooth=0), maximum(*values, smooth=0), clamp(value, lo, hi), length(*values) (square root of the summed squares; its slope stays bounded at 0, unlike sqrt) and triwave(value, period) (a continuous triangle wave between 0 and period, repeating every 2·period). Angles are radians. With numbers these functions return numbers, so math and np stay usable; np.sin(x) also builds a field. Do not rebind x, y or z in programs with fields (sdf.x stays available).
- `s.field` is a 3D shape's signed distance as a field; `s.at(fx, fy, fz)` evaluates the shape at a warped point (bends, twists, tapers). Fields read shapes, never the reverse.
- implicit(field, bounds) makes a field a solid, inside where the field is negative, within bounds ((x0, y0, z0), (x1, y1, z1)) or a Bounds. Bounds are required: enclose the solid with a little margin, and more where an outward offset, shell or smooth blend of the solid reaches past it (see Normalization); they become its bounds. Combine, transform and add only implicit() solids; a field is not a shape (a part, union or translate rejects it).
- Fields have no values while the program runs: no `if field > 0`, comparisons, `and`/`or`, builtin min/max or `%` on fields (SDFError). Use minimum, maximum, clamp and triwave. Loops, helper functions and numeric parameters are fine. Blend fields with minimum(a, b, smooth=k) and maximum(a, b, smooth=k), the polynomial blends of union(..., smooth=k) and intersection(..., smooth=k); their slope stays that of the steeper operand, while the same formula written with abs and arithmetic proves twice the slope and halves distanceQuality.
- Normalization: when the solid loads, RAD proves an upper bound L of the field's slope |grad f| over the bounds (interval arithmetic). Inside the bounds box the solid's field is max(f / L, signed distance to the box); outside it the field is known only on the box: with c the nearest point of the box and d the distance to it, the solid's field is hypot(f(c) / L, d) where f(c) > 0, else d. That is a distance bound like every other shape's; the surface is the field's zero set inside the bounds. Outward offsets, shells, smooth blends and clearance pockets that reach past the bounds see that extension instead of the field, which underestimates distances there, so they bulge past the requested surface, by up to about 0.4 of the reach for a true distance field (more where distanceQuality is lower), most where the solid lies within that reach of the bounds: make the bounds enclose the solid by more than the farthest such reach divided by distanceQuality. Where |grad f| is below L the solid's field underestimates distances: offset() outward, shell() walls, smooth blend radii and clearance pockets of the solid come out larger than requested (by up to L / |grad f|, about 1 / distanceQuality), inward offsets remove more, and rad_inspect probes report smaller distances than the true ones. distanceQuality is median |grad f| / L, 1 for a true distance such as length(x, y, z) - r. Build exact thicknesses into the field itself, or multiply requested distances by about distanceQuality.
- Shapes read far outside their own bounds by s.field or s.at() can be steeper there (a twist of an ellipsoid or a scaled shape, a loft between different profiles), which raises L: keep implicit() bounds close to the shapes a field reads. Nested twists multiply their worst-case stretches; twist once, by the combined rate, where you can.
- Every operation needs a finite slope over the bounds. Dividing by a value that can be 0, sqrt or log of a value that can reach 0 (or go negative), fractional or negative powers of such values, atan2's pole (both arguments 0) inside the bounds, and using atan2's angle across its jump (first argument changing sign while the second is negative) other than through sin or cos of whole multiples all fail the build, naming the part, the implicit() line, the operation and the region. Change that operation or shrink the bounds. An angle used only through sin or cos of whole multiples may cross atan2's jump when the bounds keep the axis (its pole) out; for a full polar pattern, make one sector away from the axis an implicit() solid and repeat it with polar_array, or use a form without atan2 (such as a polynomial in x and y).
- Check fields with rad_verify (an artifact, or a program file before building): per implicit() shape slopeBound (L), valueRange, gradientLength (min, median, max) and distanceQuality, or the problem and its region. rad_build lists customFields with slopeBound and distanceQuality. In a program, verify_field(field, bounds) (or verify_field(implicit_solid)) returns the same proof as a dict: ok, bound, value_range, gradient, quality, boxes, samples, and message and region without a bound.
- Each distinct implicit() field (a different field, or the same field over different bounds) is proven when the design loads, and one design's proofs share a fixed budget of work, which each field spends in proportion to its operations and the shapes it reads (s.field, s.at()): about 150 distinct periodic fields (lattice cells) use it all, and far fewer fields reading large shapes (unions of thousands of parts) can. Beyond that, fields load with looser slope bounds (lower distanceQuality) and slower, and a design whose fields cannot all be proven even coarsely fails to load. Make a lattice one implicit() over the whole region, place translated copies of one cell solid (copies share its proof), or grade it with one field whose thickness varies with position, rather than one implicit() per cell.
- Gyroid sheet lattice with a solid skin (10 mm cells): the sheet |g| < 0.55 is about 2·0.55 / |grad g| thick, at least 1.0 mm where g is steepest (|grad g| = √3·k). A detailed lattice exceeds the preview's triangle budget, so rad_build coarsens the preview with a warning (expected here, about 0.4 mm cells for this cube): keep the sheet at least two coarsened cells thick, or rad_validate reports pieces that split. A lattice clipped by a box alone leaves loose fragments at the faces; join it to a skin or frame. End the clipped core inside the skin (box(38) within the 2 mm wall), then intersect the result with the outer box: a smooth union of a core clipped at the outer faces bulges those faces by k/4. The labyrinths the skin encloses are sealed cavities (rad_build part cavities), which trap powder, resin or support in a print; leave openings where the material must drain.
```python
k = 2 * math.pi / 10.0                        # 10 mm cells
g = sin(k*x)*cos(k*y) + sin(k*y)*cos(k*z) + sin(k*z)*cos(k*x)
lattice = implicit(abs(g) - 0.55, bounds=((-20, -20, -20), (20, 20, 20)))
core = intersection(box(38), lattice)         # ends inside the 2 mm skin
result = intersection(box(40), union(box(40).shell(2), core, smooth=1))
```
- Warped shape: a 60 mm bar bent around a circle of radius 40 through (0, 40, 0); arc length becomes the bar's x and the radial offset its y. The bounds hold the bent bar and keep atan2's pole (x = 0, y = 40) and its jump outside.
```python
bar = box((60, 10, 10))
r = 40.0
bent = bar.at(r * atan2(x, r - y), r - length(x, r - y), z)
result = implicit(bent, bounds=((-32, -6, -6), (32, 16, 6)))
```

Assemblies and parts
```python
result = Assembly("Enclosure")                             # Assembly(name=None, id=None, color=None, print_down=None, at=None, reference=None)
case = result.folder("Case", id="case")                    # folder(name, id=None, color=None, print_down=None, at=None, reference=None) -> Assembly
base = case.add(base_shape, id="case-base", name="Base", color="#2E3440")   # add(shape, id=None, name=None, color=None, print_down=None, at=None, reference=None) -> Part
lid = case.add(lid_shape, id="case-lid", name="Lid", print_down=(0, 0, 1))
for name, px in (("left", -30), ("right", 30)):
    case.add(standoff, id=f"standoff-{name}", name=f"Standoff {name}", at=Placement(translate=(px, 0, 2)))
pcb = result.folder("PCB", id="pcb", color=(0.1, 0.45, 0.2), reference=True)   # shown and measured, never printed
pcb.add(board_shape, id="pcb-board", name="Board")
```
- Every add() is one physical part: one rigid body and one solid index, numbered in parent-before-child insertion order. A shape with disconnected pieces is still one part (RAD warns; sealed internal voids are cavities, not pieces); union pieces that belong together and add separate bodies as separate parts. Do not fuse separate components merely to organize the tree.
- id is the stable identity (rad_id): unique, nonempty, at most 1024 bytes, used verbatim. Give every part and folder you create a concise unique id (screw-front-left, screw-front-right) and keep it when renaming or moving the item. Copy existing ids exactly from the current parts layout, including prefixes such as name: or folder:. Without an id, RAD derives one from the name or tree position, which changes when items are renamed or reordered.
- name is the display label (at most 160 characters). color is (r, g, b) in 0..1 or "#RRGGBB"; folders pass colors, print_down and reference to descendants without their own.
- reference=True marks reference parts: shown in the design and used in measurements and clearance checks, but never printed, such as a circuit board and its components inside an enclosure, a battery, or a purchased part the design must fit. The 3D printing layout and exports for printing leave them out; the printing version keeps them. A part's own reference=False opts out of a reference folder.
- at is a rigid placement composed from the root down and applied to the part's shape: Placement(translate=(x, y, z), rotate=(angle, axis)) rotates about the origin, then translates; a @ b applies b first; Placement.from_matrix(m) takes a rigid 4x4 or 3x4 matrix; placement.matrix is the 4x4 row-major matrix. at rejects scale, shear and reflection; use it to instance one shape several times.
- add_assembly(sub) grafts an existing Assembly once (never into itself); add() of an Assembly without other arguments does the same. assembly.parts() lists Parts in solid-index order; a Part exposes shape, id, name, color, print_down, reference and at (read-only).
- Organize with folders: for example an enclosure's Base and Lid in a Case folder and the board and its components in a PCB folder marked reference=True.

Motion
A mechanism defines result.motion on the root Assembly; it is a Motion builder created on first use (motion on a sub-assembly is a design error).
```python
m = result.motion
m.ground(base)                                             # ground(*parts): Parts or part ids fixed to the world
m.connector("base-pivot", base, origin=(0, 0, 10))         # z_axis defaults to +Z
m.connector("arm-pivot", arm, origin=(0, 0, 10))
m.revolute("hinge", "base-pivot", "arm-pivot", name="Hinge", limits=(-90, 90))
```
- connector(id, part, origin, x_axis=None, z_axis=(0, 0, 1), frame="world") returns id: a frame on a physical part (Part or part id). Origin and axes are world coordinates of the built design; frame="part" gives them in the part's own coordinates before its at placements. Without x_axis, X is world X (world Y when Z is near X) made perpendicular to Z; given axes must be perpendicular.
- fixed(id, parent, child, name=None), revolute(id, parent, child, name=None, position=None, limits=None) and slider(id, parent, child, name=None, position=None, limits=None) return id. parent and child are connector ids created before. The child frame aligns with the parent frame, then moves by the joint position: revolute in degrees about the parent connector's Z axis, slider in mm along it. Model parts at joint position 0 with coincident connector frames; position sets the initial position and limits=(lower, upper) the travel.
- couple(id, driver, follower, type=None, ratio=None, offset=None, samples=None, period=None, advance=None) returns id and makes the follower joint a function of the driver joint: linear follower = ratio·driver + offset, or a table of [[driver, follower], ...] samples with strictly increasing drivers (at most 4,096), optionally repeating every period with the follower advancing by advance. Followers are derived: never set their position; users and tools move the driver. Coupling chains must not form a cycle.
- Coupling helpers return couple() fields, for example m.couple("mesh", "pinion", "wheel", **gear(12, 24)):
  - gear(n_driver, n_follower, internal=False): ratio -n_driver/n_follower (+ for an internal ring gear), revolute joints with parallel Z axes. Mesh involute gears of module m at centre distance m·(t1 + t2)/2. involute_gear puts a tooth on +X: for a driver at the origin and a follower on +X, rotate the follower by 180/t2 degrees when t2 is even so a tooth space faces the driver's tooth. Printed gears need backlash (about 0.1 to 0.2 mm).
  - rack(pitch_radius): 2π·r/360 mm per degree, from a pinion (revolute driver) to a rack (slider follower); a negative radius reverses the rack.
  - screw(lead): lead/360 mm per degree from a screw (revolute) to its nut (slider); a negative lead is left-handed.
  - cam(fn, start, stop, step=0.5, period=None, advance=None): a table sampling follower = fn(driver) from start to stop at most step apart. With period the table repeats; advance defaults to fn(stop) - fn(start) when the samples span a whole period, else 0.
  - geneva(slots, crank_at_center=0, step=0.5, sign=-1): an external Geneva drive, period 360: a crank (revolute driver) indexes a star wheel of N = slots slots (revolute follower, Z axes parallel) by sign·360/N per crank turn. crank_at_center is the crank joint position at which the drive pin points at the star centre. The star's modeled orientation (position 0) is at rest with the line of centres midway between two slots. The star holds 0 from crank_at_center - 180 to crank_at_center - w (w = 90 - 180/N), passes sign·180/N at crank_at_center with a slot on the line of centres holding the pin, and holds sign·360/N from crank_at_center + w. Geometry: centre distance C, crank radius A = C·sin(180°/N), star radius about C·cos(180°/N), radial slots reaching C - A minus the pin radius and clearance, slot width = pin diameter + running clearance, plus a locking disc on the crank and concave locking arcs on the star. Model the crank with its pin pointing away from the star and pass crank_at_center=180, so the modeled pose (both joints at 0) is a consistent dwell.
- A raw dictionary with the same schema (grounded, connectors, joints, couplings) may be assigned to result.motion instead; m.definition() returns it for world-frame connectors and explicitly identified parts.
- This is rigid kinematics: no collision response, contact, forces or dynamics. Closed loops are solved numerically; keep initial positions near the intended branch.
- Review mechanisms with rad_inspect kind:"motion", rad_render jointPositions (drivingJoint for closed loops) and rad_check_motion over the full range of every independent joint (a full revolution or more for indexing drives) with each pair of parts that slide past or engage each other; fix interference, unexpected contact and unreachable positions. Users move mechanisms in the viewport and the Design pane, and RAD saves joint positions per version: preserve current positions of matching joints during refinements unless asked.

3D printing version
- Every program receives rad; rad.print is True only while RAD builds the design's 3D printing version, which the user can view in place of the design and exports for printing. Read it only for printing-only geometry: a one-layer (0.2 mm) sacrificial bridge closing a bore where a counterbore steps down, so the step prints as a bridge, chamfers instead of overhanging fillets, or printing-specific clearances. Keep the intended design in the ordinary build; a program that never reads rad.print prints its ordinary geometry. Add printing geometry when the user asks or the design is meant for printing, and describe it in the summary.
- The printing version must keep the same physical parts with the same ids and solid counts; change geometry only within parts.
- RAD orients each printed part (every part except reference parts) to minimise unsupported overhangs and packs them on the build plate at z = 0. Pin an orientation with print_down=(x, y, z) on add(), folder() or Assembly(): the outward direction, in the built design's world coordinates, that faces the build plate (at placements do not rotate it). Printing-only features often depend on the orientation: a sacrificial bridge only helps where the counterbore opens toward the plate.
- Review with rad_build print=true (the printing version as its own artifact in built coordinates, inspected, measured and rendered like any artifact), rad_inspect kind:"print" and rad_render printLayout=true for the oriented, packed plate. When the final program reads rad.print, RAD also validates its printing version before accepting the design. A request selection with printVersion true belongs to the printing version, in built coordinates.

Modeling advice
- Build parts from primitives and extruded or revolved profiles combined with booleans; place them with translate, rotate or at. Pass all cutters to one difference(base, *cutters) call and all pieces to union(*pieces); loops such as s = s - hole are fine too.
- Make cutters overshoot the material by at least 1 mm (or a few preview cells) so no skin remains, and avoid faces that coincide exactly with a cutter's face.
- Rounded edges: box(..., radius=r), rect(..., radius=r) or offset(). Fillets where shapes join: union(..., smooth=r). Rounded inner corners of cuts: difference(..., smooth=r). Blends apply to every join of that call, so group the operands that should blend.
- Organic shapes (handles, grips, toys, characters): smooth unions of spheres, ellipsoids, capsules and tori with blend radii comparable to the features, then shell(), cut() or difference() as needed.
- Enclosures: model the outer solid (box(..., radius=r) or a rounded rect extruded), shell(wall) it, split base and lid with cut() or difference(), and offset() profiles inward or outward for lips, rims, gaskets and clearances. shape.offset(-t) is a surface t inside the shape. Model the enclosed PCB and its components as reference parts (reference=True on their folder), so openings, standoffs and clearances can be measured and checked against them while only the case is printed.
- Holes and bores: cylinders along the right axis (axis='x' or 'y' for horizontal bores), counterbores as stacked cylinders, countersinks as cones. Threads and knurls only when requested.
- Clearances for printed parts: 0.3 to 0.5 mm per side for running fits (pins, hinges, sliders, gears), about 0.1 to 0.2 mm for press fits, 0.4 to 0.6 mm between print-in-place parts. Walls of at least 1.2 mm (three 0.4 mm lines).
- Resolution: the preview uses one mesh cell size for the whole design (meshCellSize, about its largest extent / 256). Keep every wall, gap, pin and clearance at least two cells wide; thinner features merge or vanish in the preview and rad_validate reports them. 3MF export meshes finer (half the preview cell, 0.05 to 0.5 mm) and then merges triangles while the field stays within 0.01 mm at sampled points (slightly more between them near curved sharp edges and on scaled or blended shapes), keeping sharp edges and corners, so exported files stay compact.
- Blends, booleans, offsets, twists and non-uniform scales give distance bounds rather than true distances away from the surface. That is fine for modeling; measure the built result with RAD's tools.

Checking designs
- rad_inspect kind:"probe" with points (world mm at the artifact's pose) returns for each part the signed distance (negative inside) and outward normal; add directions to trace rays to the first surface (hit, hitDistance, hitPoint). Wall thickness: start just inside the wall and cast along the inward normal. Hole position: probe across it.
- rad_measure takes partIDs or solidIndices and up to three points such as {"point":[x, y, z],"partID":"case-lid"} (world mm; a point snaps to its part's surface, or the nearest part's).
- rad_verify reports each custom field's proven slope bound and distanceQuality (see Custom fields).
- rad_check_motion takes requiredClearance (mm) to report positions closer than a running clearance.
- rad_export_3mf writes whole, selected, individual-part, posed (jointPositions) or printing-layout (printLayout) 3MF files into the workspace, retaining each part's current color.

Reference files
Parse referenced source files with Python in the task workspace, never in the CAD program: read readPath, extract coordinates and dimensions, and write the results as literal numbers and lists into the program. Use a small s-expression tokenizer (parentheses, quoted strings, atoms) rather than fragile regular expressions. For a KiCad board (.kicad_pcb, s-expressions in mm):
- Outline: gr_line, gr_arc, gr_rect, gr_circle and gr_poly items on layer "Edge.Cuts"; chain the segments into a polygon (approximate arcs with arc_points) and extrude it by the board thickness from (general (thickness ...)), usually 1.6 mm. Inner Edge.Cuts loops are cutouts.
- Footprints: (footprint "Lib:Name" (layer "F.Cu" or "B.Cu") (at x y angle) ...) with pads (pad "1" thru_hole circle (at dx dy) (size w h) (drill d)), mounting holes (MountingHole footprints) and component envelopes from courtyard (F.CrtYd) or fabrication (F.Fab) graphics. Pad and graphic coordinates are relative to the footprint and rotated by its angle.
- KiCad's Y axis points down: map (x, y) to (x, -y), which also reverses angles; B.Cu footprints sit on the bottom side, mirrored.
- Referenced 3D component models are not available as geometry; represent components with boxes and cylinders from their outlines and known heights, and say so in the summary.
Treat DXF, SVG paths, CSV tables and other references the same way: extract numbers in the workspace, then model them.

Imported meshes
- rad_import registers a closed STL (binary or ASCII) or OBJ mesh as a design input and returns its assetID. `imported_assets[assetID]` is then that mesh as an SDF3 solid in every build that lists the assetID (rad_build assetIDs). Coordinates are the file's numbers in millimetres.
- The solid is the exact signed distance to the mesh's facets, so it combines like any shape: `imported_assets[aid].translate((0, 0, 5))`, `.rotate(90, "x")`, booleans (`bracket | boss`, `housing - imported_assets[aid].offset(0.3)` for a pocket with 0.3 mm clearance), `shell()`, `offset()` and smooth blends. Use it as a reference for fit and clearance, or as a body the design keeps.
- Facets stay facets: a curved original is an approximation, distances and measurements are to the triangles, and the preview and 3MF exports re-mesh the facets at the cell size, so facets finer than the cell are smoothed over. Never claim a smooth or exact original surface.
- Meshes must be closed (watertight), consistently oriented and manifold. Separate bodies may overlap or touch (along faces, edges or points) and are united. A shell facing inward that lies entirely within one body's solid is a cavity; one inside a cavity is an island; any other shell facing inward (an inverted body, or one crossing other shells) is turned outward, and a mesh facing inward as a whole is turned outward. Two cavities of one body that cross each other are an error. Vertices closer than 1e-6 of the mesh's size are welded, and zero-area triangles that close T-junctions (a vertex on another triangle's edge) are mended. rad_import reports remaining problems with a count and a location: holes (edges with one triangle), edges shared by more than two triangles that do not pair up (duplicated or crossing surfaces), inconsistently oriented triangles, other degenerate triangles, and shells enclosing no volume. A shell that intersects itself has wrong signs near the intersection. Repair the file with an approved tool, or explain the problem; never substitute unrelated geometry.
- radsdf also offers `mesh_solid(vertices, triangles, weld=True)`, a solid from (N, 3) vertices and (M, 3) counter-clockwise triangles computed in a program, and `load_stl(path_or_bytes)` / `load_obj(path_or_bytes)` (binary or ASCII STL; OBJ `v x y z`, with an optional weight or ignored vertex colour), which return welded `(vertices, triangles)` arrays in workspace scripts run with RAD's Python (for example to check a mesh's size before importing it). Programs cannot read files: use imported_assets for meshes.

Limits
- Program at most 200,000 UTF-8 bytes; at most 4,096 physical parts and 8,192 parts and folders, nested at most 32 levels.
- Every part must be bounded (intersect half spaces and half planes with bounded shapes), nonempty and within ±1,000,000 mm.
- The preview holds at most 1,000,000 triangles, 1,000,000 vertices and 250,000 crease segments; RAD coarsens the cell size with a warning when needed and fails designs too detailed even then.
- Polygons at most 500,000 points; arrays at most 10,000 copies; coupling tables at most 4,096 samples; at most 4,000,000 graph nodes.
- Imported meshes: at most 2,000,000 triangles and 512 MB per file, and 256 inputs per build.
